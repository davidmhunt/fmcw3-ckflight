## Goal
The user can approve every change that turns the 2-RX board into the 1-RX board in one review,
with no open question that would hold up Friday's order:
- U8 becomes the AD9237BCPZ-40 (LCSC C514275);
- the second receive chain is not fitted;
- every placed line is in JLCPCB stock for `board_quantity` boards.

Each change has its evidence, the check that covers it, and the layout work it causes. It holds
when the report builds, every change cites a downloaded datasheet page, and every choice is in
`parts/choices.json`.

- ADC rates. Raw 40 MSPS x 12 bit = 480 Mbit/s = 60.0 MB/s packed (80 MB/s as the firmware's 16 bit sign-extended words, adc.vhd lines 99-100). Raw payload alone equals the whole USB 2.0 High Speed line rate, 480 Mbits/s (ftdi p.1, p.26). I use 25 MB/s as the planning figure (the stated one) and 40 MB/s as the datasheet maximum.
- Comparison. Raw packed 60 MB/s is 240 percent of 25, 150 percent of 40, and 100 percent of the 60 MB/s bus ceiling, so even a perfect bus with zero USB overhead has no spare byte; raw 16 bit padded 80 MB/s exceeds the bus itself. Decimated: 4.0 MB/s = 16 percent of 25 (10 percent of 40); 8.0 MB/s as sent today = 32 percent of 25 (20 percent of 40). With no hardware change and 16 bit samples, the fastest stream is 12.5 MSPS (at 25 MB/s) to 20 MSPS (at 40 MB/s): the smallest integer decimation is 4 or 2, against the 20 the firmware has. So a lower decimation (for example 4, 10 MSPS, 20 MB/s) is a firmware change, not a board change.
- FPGA buffers (U30 XC7A100T, ds180 p.3 Table 4): 135 blocks of 36 Kb = 4,860 Kb (607.5 kbyte); the as-ordered XC7A35T has 1,800 Kb. The firmware's three streaming FIFOs (xci files at cb7f097): ADC FIFO 2048 x 32 bit (64 Kb, common clock, written only during the ramp), USB TX FIFO 16384 x 8 bit (128 Kb), USB RX FIFO 1024 x 8 bit (8 Kb): 200 Kb, 4.1 percent of the XC7A100T. A decimated 1 ms chirp is 2000 samples + the 0xC8C8C8C8 header word (control.vhd line 265) = 2001 words in the 2048-word ADC FIFO (1.02 ms of data at 2 MSPS), so the whole chirp is buffered and the USB side drains it during the gap. The microblaze subsystem's own block RAM is not counted (its size is not in the files read).
- Raw chirp in the FPGA: 1 ms x 40 MSPS = 40,000 samples = 625 Kb at 16 bit (468.75 Kb packed 12 bit) = 12.9 percent of the XC7A100T block RAM (34.7 percent of the XC7A35T). Real-time raw SAR at a continuous 1 ms chirp cannot be sustained through this USB path.

- ADC rates. Raw 40 MSPS x 12 bit = 480 Mbit/s = 60.0 MB/s packed (80 MB/s as the firmware's 16 bit sign-extended words, adc.vhd lines 99-100). Raw payload alone equals the whole USB 2.0 High Speed line rate, 480 Mbits/s (ftdi p.1, p.26). Decimated by 20 (fir_compiler_0.xci Decimation_Rate 20, 21 taps): 2 MSPS; 4.0 MB/s as 16 bit single channel, 8.0 MB/s as the firmware sends it today (control.vhd line 295, 32 bit word = channel A and B, 16 bit each). adc.vhd lines 123-138: even with the FIR switched off a valid pulse comes every 20 clocks, so the firmware has no 40 MSPS output path at all.
- FT2232H synchronous FIFO (channel A, single-channel mode; the board uses it, usb_sync.vhd, 60 MHz usb_clk): datasheet p.1 "transfers upto 40 Mbytes/Sec"; p.26 "greater than 25 Mbytes/second over a synchronous parallel FIFO interface"; p.32 the chip drives a 60 MHz CLKOUT, one byte per clock on an 8 bit bus (p.12 Table 3.6, p.31 Table 4.1 t1 = 16.67 ns) = 60 MB/s bus ceiling, burst on every clock while TXE# is low (p.32 section 4.4.2); 4 kbyte TX and RX buffers per interface (p.26). I use 25 MB/s as the planning figure (the stated one) and 40 MB/s as the datasheet maximum.
- Comparison. Raw packed 60 MB/s is 240 percent of 25, 150 percent of 40, and 100 percent of the 60 MB/s bus ceiling, so even a perfect bus with zero USB overhead has no spare byte; raw 16 bit padded 80 MB/s exceeds the bus itself. Decimated: 4.0 MB/s = 16 percent of 25 (10 percent of 40); 8.0 MB/s as sent today = 32 percent of 25 (20 percent of 40). With no hardware change and 16 bit samples, the fastest stream is 12.5 MSPS (at 25 MB/s) to 20 MSPS (at 40 MB/s): the smallest integer decimation is 4 or 2, against the 20 the firmware has. So a lower decimation (for example 4, 10 MSPS, 20 MB/s) is a firmware change, not a board change.
- FPGA buffers (U30 XC7A100T, ds180 p.3 Table 4): 135 blocks of 36 Kb = 4,860 Kb (607.5 kbyte); the as-ordered XC7A35T has 1,800 Kb. The firmware's three streaming FIFOs (xci files at cb7f097): ADC FIFO 2048 x 32 bit (64 Kb, common clock, written only during the ramp), USB TX FIFO 16384 x 8 bit (128 Kb), USB RX FIFO 1024 x 8 bit (8 Kb): 200 Kb, 4.1 percent of the XC7A100T. A decimated 1 ms chirp is 2000 samples + the 0xC8C8C8C8 header word (control.vhd line 265) = 2001 words in the 2048-word ADC FIFO (1.02 ms of data at 2 MSPS), so the whole chirp is buffered and the USB side drains it during the gap. The microblaze subsystem's own block RAM is not counted (its size is not in the files read).
- Raw chirp in the FPGA: 1 ms x 40 MSPS = 40,000 samples = 625 Kb at 16 bit (468.75 Kb packed 12 bit) = 12.9 percent of the XC7A100T block RAM (34.7 percent of the XC7A35T). So the FPGA can hold a raw chirp (a firmware change, no hardware), but that is store and forward, not streaming: draining 80 kbyte through the FT2232H takes 3.2 ms at 25 MB/s (2.0 ms at 40 MB/s), so at most about 312 chirps/s (500) at 31 percent (50 percent) duty with a 1 ms chirp. Real-time raw SAR at a continuous 1 ms chirp cannot be sustained through this USB path.
- Firmware ceiling, not a hardware limit: control.vhd moves one byte per 4 clocks (TX_PREP_BYTE, TX_WRITE_BYTE, two for the ack) plus 2 per word, 18 clocks per 32 bit word at 40 MHz = 8.9 MB/s, only 11 percent above the 8.0 MB/s the 2 MSPS stream needs; usb_sync.vhd writes one byte per 5 usb_clk = 12 MB/s. Counted from the state machines, not simulated, so treat as a rough figure. Sending 16 bit single-channel words (4 MB/s) in the 1-RX firmware, which the later goal does anyway for one ADC channel, halves the load. Both are well under the FT2232H's 25 MB/s.
